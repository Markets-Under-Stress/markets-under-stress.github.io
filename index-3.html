How fifteen geopolitical, political, health and economic shocks changed investable assets, and why a falling price was not always an opportunity.

Markets move for many reasons. Timing alone does not prove causation.

Four findings

Access comes firstRussia’s 2022 quoted prices were not generally realizable by foreign investors after closures and sanctions. E15 S29
Claims, not sectorsBroad markets recovered after 1929 and 2008; failed banks’ original shares did not. E02 E11
One shock, opposite cash flowsOil rose during the 1973 embargo while fuel users suffered. The broader bear market had other causes too. E04
Recovery has a basisThe Dow’s nominal price recovery after its 22.6% Black Monday fall says nothing universal about real total returns or failed claims. O016

E01 · Panic of 1873

Trigger: 18 Sep 1873 · Confidence: moderate · S01 S02 S03

Observed. Jay Cooke & Co. failed and the NYSE closed ten days. “Long Depression” is disputed; NBER’s defensible U.S. contraction is October 1873 to March 1879.

Possible mechanism. Railroad-finance stress restricted credit; deflation raised real debt burdens.

Limits/confounders. Monthly prices, heterogeneous railroad claims and later panics prevent a clean event estimate.

E02 · Great Depression

Trigger: 3 Sep 1929 · Confidence: moderate · S02 S03 S04

Observed. The economic peak preceded the October crash; the crash alone did not cause the Depression. Banking failures, monetary contraction, deflation and collapsing output followed.

Possible mechanism. Earnings fell, claims failed and risk premiums rose.

Limits/confounders. Interacting banking, policy and real-economy mechanisms prevent a single-cause claim.

Trigger: 1 Sep 1939 · Confidence: weak · S03 S05 S06

Benjamin Graham during the Great Depression

Record first

Graham’s own account reports that the Benjamin Graham Joint Account lost about 70% cumulatively from 1929 through 1932. S31 That belongs before the later principles. The Shiller composite lost about 84.8% from September 1929 to June 1932 on a nominal price basis, which is not a like-for-like partnership total return. O004

Leverage, withdrawals and illiquidity can end participation before recovery. Statistical cheapness did not guarantee solvency.

Published response

Security Analysis (1934), with David Dodd, emphasized evidence-based intrinsic value, balance-sheet analysis and margin of safety. S32 Later, The Intelligent Investor developed defensive-investor principles. S33

A net-net compares market value with current assets minus all liabilities. It is a screen, not proof of safety. Later writing is not evidence of pre-crash action.

Warren Buffett and geopolitics

Documented facts

Buffett wrote in 1994 that Berkshire would ignore political and economic forecasts, naming wars, oil shocks, political change and Black Monday. S34

The 2007 letter says Berkshire invested $488 million in PetroChina in 2002–03 and sold for about $4 billion after oil, reserves and valuation changed. It documents valuation, not a geopolitical forecast. S35

Operating exposure

Berkshire estimated about $2.2 billion of 9/11 insurance losses and acknowledged underpriced, aggregated terrorism exposure. S20 In 2014 Buffett said diversified American business ownership endured the Depression and two world wars. S36

Inference: public evidence supports security-level valuation and operating-risk management more strongly than geopolitical market timing. This is not a universal law for every country or claim.

Method and limitations

Return rules

Default is nominal total return in local currency. Where checked total-return data were unavailable, price return is explicitly labeled. Dates use the last valid observation on or before start and first on or after end. Percentages display one decimal; calculation notes retain more precision. No price is interpolated through closure. Recovery must use the original basis.

Limits

Frequency ranges from daily to monthly. Pre-1957 data are called a Shiller U.S. equity composite, never the S&P 500. Price returns omit dividends and inflation. Currency matters. Missing histories remain unavailable. Confounders, survivorship bias, index changes, sanctions, illiquidity and capital controls limit inference.
